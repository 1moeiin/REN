{% load static i18n site_extras %} {% comment %} Cinematic hero: one continuous camera move down the building, scrubbed by scroll. home.html includes this when a hero image is uploaded; the motion lives in static/js/cinematic-hero.js, and this file is only the set. .cine__track is the scroll track, and its height is the length of the shot. The stage inside it is CSS-sticky rather than JS-pinned, so touch scrolling stays native. Layers, back to front: camera the render (#hero-photo), carried past its own edges in colours sampled from the image (so ultra-wide screens still read as sky), plus a sky-only copy JS adds on larger screens that drifts slower than the tower — the parallax that makes one flat image feel deep haze atmosphere that thickens as the camera nears the cloud layer clouds far banks → wordmark → fog → mid banks → near veils, so REN sits inside the clouds and is genuinely occluded by them grade vignette, and the ground fade the shot settles into title the headline, calls to action and stats (_hero_copy.html), set in the sky above the clouds: they ride up with it as the descent begins and are gone before the deck The steps (_process.html) come after the track but are pulled up over its last screen, so below the clouds they rise into the shot as the camera reaches the entrance, then carry on up the page. They scroll natively, so however long they run on a phone they never reach back into the clouds. Without the film (no JS, reduced motion) the stage is one still screen, the render with the title over it, and the steps simply follow it on ink. The --cine-* fractions describe this particular render: the tower's flanks, the crown and the horizon (for the sky mask). data-focus-end is the height of the entrance, where the shot comes to rest. Retune them if the photo changes; the sky drift also switches itself off when the image has no clean sky where the mask expects it, so a different photo degrades gracefully. Clouds are lazy with low fetch priority: they never compete with the render for first paint, and CSS keeps them display:none wherever they won't animate (reduced motion, no JS), so those visitors never download them. {% endcomment %}
